# GLP-1 User Research Brief
## For Alcami Elements — Meta Ad Creative Development
*Researched June 2026 via Firecrawl / web sources*

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY FOR AD STRATEGY

GLP-1 users represent a uniquely self-aware wellness audience. They are already invested in their health, already paying for medical support, already tracking their body's signals. What they are *not* getting from most wellness brands: acknowledgment of the specific texture of their daily life. They are eating less but worrying more — about muscle loss, nutrient gaps, fatigue, and whether they are doing this "right." They are not a weight-loss audience. They are a *precision body management* audience. That is Alcami's register.

The brand constraint that matters most: no drug brand names in creative. This is both a policy rule and a creative opportunity. The language GLP-1 users use *among themselves* is not "Ozempic users." It is "people on this journey," "the medication," "my GLP-1 routine." Alcami copy can speak to the experience — the quiet mornings, the changed relationship with coffee, the new need for sustained clean energy — without ever touching a drug name.

## 1. THE DAILY PHYSICAL EXPERIENCE

### Morning
- Appetite is dramatically suppressed, especially in the first hours after waking. Many users skip or delay breakfast entirely — not out of discipline but because they genuinely have no hunger signal.
- **Coffee relationship changes.** This is the single most distinctive physical shift worth noting for Alcami: many GLP-1 users report that coffee becomes aversive or nauseating. Reddit data from `/r/pourover` and multiple medical sources confirm users saying "my coffee tasted awful," linking it to GLP-1's gastrointestinal effects and altered taste sensitivity. Users specifically describe the combination of GLP-1 nausea + coffee's acidity as unpleasant. Many are actively looking for a warm morning ritual that doesn't upset their stomach.
- Fatigue is common, especially in dose-escalation weeks. Causes: rapid calorie reduction, low protein intake, dehydration/electrolyte imbalance, blood sugar shifts.
- Nausea peaks in the morning for many users; eating on an empty stomach (including drinking coffee on empty) worsens it.

### Afternoon
- The traditional afternoon energy crash may be amplified by eating too little at lunch (often a consequence of suppressed appetite and a small meal).
- Brain fog is reported by some users, connected to low caloric intake and nutrient insufficiency (particularly B12, iron, electrolytes).
- Constipation is common mid-day, as GLP-1s slow gastric emptying.
- Users report needing to be intentional about eating something — "I have to set alarms to remind myself to eat."

### Evening
- Later in the day, some appetite returns. The medication effect is not constant — it tends to peak and trough.
- Social eating situations become complicated: portions that were normal now cause discomfort.
- Many users report not missing food for itself, but missing the social ritual around food (sharing a meal, the comfort of a full plate).

### Energy patterns overall
- Energy is described as "unpredictable" especially in the first months. Some days fine, some days depleted.
- The fatigue is qualitatively different from normal tiredness — more like a "flat battery" feeling than sleepiness.
- Users on forums actively discuss how to maintain energy without food, turning to electrolytes, protein shakes, and supplements.

## 2. THE EMOTIONAL AND PSYCHOLOGICAL EXPERIENCE

### "Food noise" — the defining concept
"Food noise" emerged from patient conversations, not clinical literature. It refers to the constant internal dialogue about food — what to eat next, whether to snack, cravings even without physical hunger. Multiple sources describe this as a lifelong experience for many users that GLP-1s simply *turn down*.

**Representative community expressions:**
- "Food noise was a 9/10 before. It's a 4/10 now."
- "I don't think about food the same way."
- "For some, the absence of food noise was more significant than the weight loss." — Dr. Andrea Bedrosian, Northwell Health
- "The constant and oppressive thoughts about food are gone."
- "Meals became something I respond to physically, not mentally obsess over."

This is liberation. But it comes with complexity.

### What is liberating
- Not thinking about food constantly. The mental bandwidth freed up is described as profound.
- Making food choices that feel neutral and rational, not compulsive.
- Eating smaller portions and feeling satisfied — not deprived.
- The feeling that "healthy habits feel more effortless."
- Body confidence and control.

### What is hard / what they worry about
- **Muscle loss.** This is the #1 wellness concern in GLP-1 communities. Users eat less, but they know that rapid weight loss includes muscle loss. "Losing weight is one thing, but having that weight come from lean muscle tissue is a concerning risk." (Nutrabio) — this fear drives heavy supplement research.
- **Are they getting enough nutrients?** Eating 800–1200 calories a day means even nutrient-dense eating may leave gaps. Users actively track: protein, B12, vitamin D, iron, calcium, electrolytes.
- **The GI side effects** — nausea, constipation, bloating, acid reflux. These affect quality of life significantly. Managing them is a daily challenge.
- **Emotional eating patterns persist.** GLP-1s reduce the *physical* hunger signal but not the psychological drivers. Users report that emotional eating urges still exist — they just no longer have the physical hunger to rationalize them. This creates a new kind of internal conflict.
- **What happens when I stop?** Anxiety about weight regain, loss of the "food noise off" feeling. This creates strong ongoing adherence motivation.
- **Missing the social side of food.** Going to a dinner party and not being hungry. Ordering from a menu and having half a dish. Social rituals built around eating now feel complicated.
- **Coffee.** Specifically, users who were heavy coffee drinkers are looking for alternatives. Coffee's acidity, combined with GLP-1 nausea, makes it a common trigger. Users ask "what can I drink in the morning instead?"

## 3. THE MOST VISUALLY DISTINCTIVE GLP-1 MOMENTS

These are the scenes GLP-1 community members post, describe, and share. Ranked by ad-creative potential for Alcami:

### Moment 1: The half-eaten plate
Exactly half a restaurant or home-cooked meal remains, perfectly good food, just — done. A fork set down mid-plate. Not sad; almost peaceful. This is the visual shorthand for "I can't believe I'm satisfied already." Universally recognized in the community. Powerful for framing Alcami as a complement to the new eating pattern.

### Moment 2: The coffee cup that didn't get finished
A cold, nearly full cup of coffee on a desk or kitchen counter. Steam long gone. The user switched to something else or simply couldn't finish it. This is specifically resonant for the Alcami blend — a warm ritual that doesn't cause nausea, doesn't over-caffeinate a body whose caffeine tolerance has changed.

### Moment 3: The morning alarm for eating
A phone screen showing a recurring reminder: "Eat something." Not "time to cook" — a reminder to eat at all. This image captures the cognitive inversion that GLP-1 creates: eating is now intentional work, not a default drive.

### Moment 4: The supplement shelf / morning routine spread
Multiple bottles lined up: protein powder, electrolytes, multivitamin, B12, creatine, probiotics. The "GLP-1 ritual" has grown into its own stack. This is the most supplement-purchase-motivated moment — users see their peers' supplement routines and make decisions. Alcami belongs in this frame.

### Moment 5: The quiet kitchen — morning, no hunger
Standing in a kitchen. Morning light. Not hungry, not stressed about food, just... quiet. The internal food noise is absent. This is the emotional heart of the GLP-1 experience — the absence of something that was previously consuming. An Alcami moment if the copy names what that quiet *is*.

### Moment 6: The mid-afternoon wall — low energy, not hungry
Sitting at a desk at 3pm, tired, low, but no appetite to signal "eat something." This is the energy gap problem. Users know they need fuel but their body isn't asking for it. This is Alcami's direct territory — calm, sustained energy without caffeine, without a heavy meal.

### Moment 7: The pen
The injection pen (autoinjector) itself is the most recognizable visual symbol of being on the GLP-1 journey. It appears in community posts, before/after stories, "medication day" content. A pen + a morning ritual product creates an instant visual identity for the audience.

## 4. SUPPLEMENTS AND WELLNESS PRODUCTS GLP-1 USERS ARE ACTIVELY SEEKING

This is highly actionable for Alcami positioning.

### The Top-10 list (per a doctor-reviewed YouTube video with 13K+ views, plus GoodRx, WeightWatchers, NutraBio clinical sources)

**Priority tier (every GLP-1 user needs these):**
1. **Protein powder** — #1 priority. Muscle preservation. Users try to hit 100–130g protein/day on 800–1200 calories. This is hard to do from food alone.
2. **Electrolytes** — Dehydration and sodium/potassium loss from reduced eating and nausea/vomiting are common. Hydration products are actively purchased.
3. **Multivitamin** — Insurance against nutrient gaps from reduced food volume. B vitamins, iron, D, A, E are the primary gaps.
4. **Vitamin B12** — Specifically linked to semaglutide reducing B12 absorption. Energy, metabolism, nerve function.
5. **Vitamin D + Calcium** — Bone density loss is a documented concern with rapid weight loss.

**Secondary tier (widely discussed in community):**
6. **Magnesium** — Constipation management and sleep quality.
7. **Fiber supplement** — GLP-1s slow gastric emptying; fiber helps digestive regularity.
8. **Omega-3s** — Cardiovascular and inflammation support.
9. **Probiotics / gut support** — GI side effects drive strong demand for gut health products. This is a direct Alcami connection.
10. **Creatine** — Muscle preservation during weight loss. Increasingly recommended by medical providers.

### What this means for Alcami
Alcami's formulation addresses several of these organically:
- **Gut balance/immunity** — directly maps to the gut health concern
- **Sustained clean energy without caffeine** — maps to the fatigue + caffeine-sensitivity gap
- **Adaptogens for stress and cortisol** — maps to the psychological stress of the GLP-1 journey
- **Coconut milk powder base** — easy to digest, no nausea trigger

The natural positioning angle: Alcami is *not* a GLP-1 supplement. It is a daily ritual that fits the new life GLP-1 is creating — calm energy, gut support, no caffeine crash — without requiring the user to think of themselves as a patient or supplement-stacker.

## 5. HOW WELLNESS BRANDS ARE CURRENTLY MARKETING TO GLP-1 USERS

### The landscape (per ATTN Agency, Mintel, healthcare marketing research)
- The GLP-1 supporting supplement market is a rapidly growing space. Brands are rushing to attach "GLP-1 support" labels to protein powders, multivitamins, and meal replacement products.
- Three main approaches brands are taking:
  1. **Direct GLP-1 labeling** — "GLP-1 Support Formula," "For users on semaglutide" — clinical, product-first
  2. **Side effect targeting** — "Manage nausea," "Fight muscle loss," "Stay nourished while losing weight" — problem-solution
  3. **Lifestyle/ritual framing** — "The wellness routine built for your journey" — brand-forward, aspirational

### What's working (per agency analysis and brand observations)
- Empathy-first messaging that acknowledges the complexity — not just "you're losing weight, great!" but "this is a big change and your body has new needs"
- Community-tone content; showing real daily moments, not clinical before/afters
- Educational authority — brands that explain *why* nutrients are depleted earn trust faster than those that just list benefits
- Before/after framing done right: not body comparison, but *daily life* comparison ("what a morning felt like before" vs. "what it feels like now")

### What feels tone-deaf (per Mintel and consumer psychology research)
- **Leading with weight loss** — GLP-1 users bristle at being reduced to their weight. They are already managing weight; they want to feel *well* while doing it.
- **Celebrity endorsement plays** — the GLP-1 community is acutely aware of celebrity Ozempic use (and judgment). Leading with celebrity credibility reads as exploitative or shallow.
- **Fearmongering about side effects** — "Ozempic face," "muscle wasting," "hair loss" as hooks. The community is already anxious about this; scaring them further without offering genuine support is seen as predatory.
- **Implying the medication is a shortcut** — this community is intensely self-aware about how their choices are perceived. Anything that signals "you took the easy way" lands badly.
- **Treating them as a patient audience** — clinical, sterile, medical-tone copy. These users have enough clinical in their life. They want brand relationships that feel human.
- **Generic wellness positioning** — "Support your wellness journey." Vague copy with no specific understanding of the GLP-1 experience doesn't land. Users can tell immediately if a brand gets their actual daily reality or is just keyword-farming.

### What the Facebook/Instagram ad restriction means
Meta has stricter content review for weight loss and health claims. Before-and-after body images require approval. This environment makes *lifestyle* and *ritual* creative — Alcami's natural territory — more viable than clinical comparison approaches.

## 6. COMMUNITY LANGUAGE

### Terms that feel native
- **"food noise"** — the defining phrase. Use it or approximate it in copy.
- **"on this medication" / "on my GLP-1"** — generic phrasing users use to describe themselves. No drug brand names in community discourse when being community-first.
- **"the injection pen" / "pen day" / "shot day"** — weekly injection ritual language.
- **"dose week" / "higher dose week"** — the escalation schedule is a known rhythm; the first days after a new dose can be rougher.
- **"brain fog"** — energy and cognitive clarity concerns are frequently named this way.
- **"hitting my protein"** — protein intake tracking is a shared obsession.
- **"not hungry but I need to eat"** — describes the cognitive experience of suppressed appetite requiring intentional eating.
- **"GLP-1 journey"** — the preferred self-descriptor for the experience.
- **"NSV" (Non-Scale Victory)** — community term for wins that aren't about weight (energy, mood, sleep quality, relationship with food).
- **"the quiet"** — some users describe the absence of food noise as simply quiet. This is emotionally loaded and positive.
- **"reset" / "new relationship with food"** — frames the medication as a reset, not a crutch.

### Hashtags in community use
`#GLP1` · `#GLP1Journey` · `#OzempicJourney` (drug-name adjacent but community-standard) · `#WegovyJourney` · `#Tirzepatide` · `#FoodNoise` · `#GLP1Community` · `#GLP1Support` · `#NonScaleVictory` · `#NSV` · `#ProteinFirst` · `#MuscleNotFat` · `#GLP1Supplements`

Note: TikTok has been actively suppressing the `#GLP1` hashtag on some accounts (reported June 2026), suggesting platform moderation is tightening. Meta/Instagram is the more stable community platform for this audience currently.

### Language that reads as clinical or exploitative
- Using drug brand names in any form that reads like a claim ("For Ozempic users...") — triggers association with pharmaceutical marketing
- "Ozempic face" / "Ozempic body" — loaded, judgment-adjacent terms
- "Weight loss drug users" — clinical framing that lacks empathy
- "Boost your GLP-1 naturally" — natural-supplement brands using this angle have faced backlash for implying their product is equivalent to medication
- Percentages and specific outcome claims without substantiation
- "Fix" language ("fix your metabolism," "fix muscle loss") — implies something is broken

## 7. SPECIFICALLY FOR ALCAMI: THE ANGLE THAT FITS

Alcami's product profile maps to the GLP-1 user experience in ways that are natural, not forced:

| GLP-1 user experience | Alcami connection |
|---|---|
| Coffee is nauseating or aversive | Alcami is a warm, creamy, low-acid morning ritual — the thing that replaces coffee without asking you to give up the ritual |
| Fatigue without appetite signal | Sustained, non-caffeinated energy — adaptogens give a lift that doesn't spike and crash |
| Gut distress (nausea, bloating, constipation) | "Gut Balance + Immunity" is literally on the pouch |
| Feeling depleted, nutrient anxiety | 9 adaptogens, 10:1 concentrated extracts — dense nutrition in a small daily dose |
| Rebuilding a new relationship with morning | The morning ritual is now intentional — Alcami is the ritual they choose, not the one they default to |
| Wanting to feel well, not just thin | Alcami's positioning is peak performance and vitality — not weight loss |

**The Alcami angle that doesn't overstep:**
Alcami never claims to be a GLP-1 supplement, never uses drug names, never makes weight or metabolism claims. The creative simply lives in the daily experience — specifically the morning moment, the changed relationship with coffee, the need for clean consistent energy — and lets the product be the answer to what the viewer is already feeling.

**Creative-specific constraint reminder for Alcami `glp1_v1` batch:**
Per CLAUDE.md and current campaign rules:
- Drug brand names (Ozempic/Wegovy/Mounjaro/Zepbound/semaglutide) are hard-banned from any rendered text — validator will error
- Second-person GLP-1 phrasing ("your GLP-1" / "are you on GLP-1") triggers Personal-Attributes warning — use third-person ("the GLP-1 ritual," "people on this routine")
- Class term "GLP-1" may appear in primary-text copy (in image: third-person only, never second-person)
- No food-adjacent objects except the warm cup. No plates, no food imagery.
- Emotion is uniform-gentle. Frames diverge on ROOM + TIME + ACTION.

## 8. VISUAL CREATIVE DIRECTION — SUMMARY

**Frames that land for this audience:**

1. **The quiet kitchen, 7am** — no hunger, no noise, warm mug with steam. No food. Just the ritual. Alcami pouch. The caption names what it is: "I don't think about food the same way anymore."

2. **The desk at 3pm** — mid-afternoon light, laptop, a mug. Not the third coffee. The person is present, not fighting an energy crash. Copy: "The 3pm I stopped dreading."

3. **The morning routine counter** — supplement bottles, the pen, a warm cup. Alcami among a considered morning stack. The message is: this is the ritual I built. Not a medical routine. A self-care routine.

4. **The cold coffee** — a full, cold, abandoned coffee cup. And next to it, a warm steaming Alcami mug. No text needed. Or one line: "Traded the crash for the ritual."

5. **The comparison column** — old morning (coffee, jitter, crash) vs. new morning (Alcami, calm, sustained). Specific, factual, not aggressive.

**Visual notes:**
- These are all calm-register scenes, not loud/energetic
- Warm color temperatures (gold, cream, morning light)
- The product must be the resolution — not an afterthought in the corner
- No people who read as ill, medically compromised, or in distress
- The warm cup is the only food-adjacent object. No plates.
